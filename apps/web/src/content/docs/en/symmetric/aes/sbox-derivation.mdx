## Two ingredients, one table

The 256 entries of the S-box are not random. Each one is computed by the same rule
(FIPS 197, §5.1.1):

<Formula caption="The S-box rule, FIPS 197 §5.1.1 (Equation 5.1). A is an 8×8 bit matrix.">
{`S(x) = A · x⁻¹ ⊕ {63}          (x⁻¹ in GF(2⁸), with {00}⁻¹ = {00})`}
</Formula>

1. The **multiplicative inverse** x⁻¹ in the finite field GF(2⁸) makes the S-box strongly
   non-linear.
2. The **affine map** — the bit matrix A followed by XOR with `{63}` — hides the clean algebra of
   the inverse and removes fixed points.

Each ingredient alone would be weak; together they give a table that resists the standard
statistical attacks (Daemen & Rijmen, §3.4.1). This page takes the rule apart, one byte at a time.

</KeyFacts>

## Why inversion, why affine

**Inversion.** For x ↦ x⁻¹ in GF(2⁸), a fixed non-zero input difference leads to the same output
difference for at most 4 of the 256 inputs, and the nonlinearity is 112: no linear approximation has
correlation above 2⁻³ (Nyberg 1993). These are the best values known for 8-bit permutations.

<WhyBox constant="A" title="the affine bit matrix">
  Inversion alone has a short formula, x • x⁻¹ = `{01}`, that interpolation attacks exploit
  (Jakobsen & Knudsen 1997). A is linear over GF(2) but not over GF(2⁸), so S becomes a nine-term
  polynomial of degree 254 (Daemen & Rijmen, §3.4.1) with unchanged statistics.
</WhyBox>

<WhyBox constant="{63}" title="affine constant">
  x⁻¹ fixes `{00}` and `{01}`. The constant gives S **no fixed point**, S(x) ≠ x, and **no opposite
  fixed point**, S(x) ≠ x̄ (Daemen & Rijmen, §3.4.1).
</WhyBox>

**Inverse S-box.** Undo the steps in reverse (FIPS 197, §5.3.2): XOR `{63}`, apply A⁻¹, then
invert. Example: S⁻¹(`{ed}`) = `{53}`.

<Formula caption="Inverse S-box, FIPS 197 §5.3.2, in rotate form (A⁻¹ rotates by 1, 3 and 6; A⁻¹·{63} = {05}).">
{`S⁻¹(y) = (A⁻¹ · (y ⊕ {63}))⁻¹
       = ((y ⋘ 1) ⊕ (y ⋘ 3) ⊕ (y ⋘ 6) ⊕ {05})⁻¹`}
</Formula>

<Lens level="cryptographer">

<Formula caption="S as a polynomial over GF(2⁸), by Lagrange interpolation (Daemen & Rijmen, §3.4.1). Each Boolean output bit has algebraic degree 7, the maximum for a permutation of GF(2)⁸.">
{`S(x) = {05}•x²⁵⁴ ⊕ {09}•x²⁵³ ⊕ {f9}•x²⁵¹ ⊕ {25}•x²⁴⁷ ⊕ {f4}•x²³⁹
     ⊕ {01}•x²²³ ⊕ {b5}•x¹⁹¹ ⊕ {8f}•x¹²⁷ ⊕ {63}`}
</Formula>

</Lens>

## Table or circuit

Software stores S and S⁻¹ as two 256-byte tables; a lookup indexed by a secret byte can leak through
the cache. The derivation offers a way out: bitsliced and hardware designs compute x⁻¹ as a small
logic circuit, splitting GF(2⁸) into smaller tower fields (Canright 2005), so no table is needed. See
[Memory & hardware](../memory-and-hardware/).

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection part="break">

## Mallory flattens the S-box

Mallory drops the inverse and keeps only S(x) = A·x ⊕ `{63}`. Now every input difference maps to
**one** output difference with certainty (256 of 256, not 4), and the nonlinearity falls to 0 — we
computed both. ShiftRows, MixColumns and AddRoundKey are affine too, so the whole cipher becomes
one affine map over GF(2): about 129 known pairs (enough to be affinely independent) and Gaussian
elimination reveal an equivalent cipher without ever finding the key (SubBytes is the only
non-linear step, FIPS 197, §5.1.1). A later playground will let you swap S-boxes and run the attack
yourself.

## Check yourself

<CheckQuestion number={1} question="What is S({01})?" options={['{01}', '{63}', '{7c}', '{8d}']} answer={2}>
  `{01}` is its own inverse. The affine matrix turns `{01}` into `{1f}`, and `{1f} ⊕ {63} = {7c}`
  (FIPS 197, Figure 7, row 0, column 1).
</CheckQuestion>

<CheckQuestion number={2} question="What does the inverse S-box return for {63}?" options={['{00}', '{01}', '{63}', '{9c}']} answer={0}>
  `{63} ⊕ {63} = {00}`, A⁻¹ keeps `{00}`, and `{00}⁻¹ = {00}` by convention. So S⁻¹(`{63}`) = `{00}`,
  matching S(`{00}`) = `{63}` (FIPS 197, §5.3.2).
</CheckQuestion>

<CheckQuestion
  number={3}
  question="Fix a non-zero input difference Δ. For at most how many of the 256 inputs x does S(x) ⊕ S(x ⊕ Δ) take the same value?"
  options={['1', '2', '4', '256']}
  answer={2}
>
  At most 4, the differential uniformity of inversion in GF(2⁸) (Nyberg 1993). The affine map does
  not change it. An affine-only S-box would reach 256.
</CheckQuestion>

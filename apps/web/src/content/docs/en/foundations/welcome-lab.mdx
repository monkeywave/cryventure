AES encrypts a 16-byte block by running it through several rounds. Each round mixes the
**state** with a **round key** and scrambles it further. It all starts with `AddRoundKey` (XOR the
state with the key) followed by `SubBytes` (swap every byte using the S-box).

Step through the official FIPS 197 example below. Use the buttons, drag the timeline, or click
into the lab and press <kbd>←</kbd> / <kbd>→</kbd>. Change the key or the plaintext and the lab
recomputes everything — the link in your address bar remembers where you are.
